Highlands Ranch by Quadrant and Age

Northridge is the community's oldest section, built roughly 1984 to 1993 with generous lot sizes and a mix of traditional, contemporary, and Tudor-style homes. Original plumbing infrastructure is more common here than anywhere else in Highlands Ranch, which means galvanized or early-generation copper is still a real possibility on a service call.

Southridge and the eastern sections represent later-phase construction into the 1990s and 2000s, generally with copper or early PEX plumbing in better condition. Westridge, including the BackCountry gated community, represents some of the newest construction in Highlands Ranch, built on hillside terrain with modern PEX systems as standard.

Because the whole community was built out from a single master plan starting in 1981, Highlands Ranch's plumbing ages more predictably by section than Centennial's patchwork of pre-existing neighborhoods, which makes an address-based estimate of likely pipe material fairly reliable here.

The historic Highlands Ranch Mansion, built in 1891 long before the modern master-planned community existed, is a reminder that this land has a much older story than its 1980s subdivision names suggest, even though the residential plumbing itself is comparatively young.

We treat Highlands Ranch as a full-community coverage area rather than favoring one quadrant, since Douglas County permitting and the shared Metro District water service apply the same way regardless of which section you call from.

Our routes also reach south toward Castle Rock's own 80104 and west toward Centennial's 80122, so a Highlands Ranch dispatch fits naturally into our regular daily coverage rather than requiring a special trip.

That consistency is worth mentioning because a few service providers focus mainly on the newer sections and treat Northridge's older infrastructure as an afterthought. We do not; the older section is exactly where our repiping and slab leak experience matters most.

A Northridge home with its original 1980s plumbing deserves the same diagnostic attention as a new BackCountry build, not a rushed assumption that "it's an old house, what do you expect."

We would rather spend a few extra minutes confirming the actual cause than fall back on that kind of shrug, regardless of which quadrant the call comes from.

That standard holds whether you are calling from a Northridge street lined with mature trees or a newer BackCountry cul-de-sac still smelling like fresh paint.

Tell us your quadrant when you call and we can already start narrowing down likely causes before we arrive.

Local Governance

A Note on Permitting Here

Highlands Ranch is unincorporated Douglas County, not a home-rule town like Castle Rock, so plumbing permits here go through Douglas County directly rather than a town building department. The Highlands Ranch Metropolitan District, formed in 1982, handles water and sanitation service across the community.
