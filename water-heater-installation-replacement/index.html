Choosing a Replacement for Castle Rock's Water

Castle Rock Water reports moderately hard to hard water in the 108 to 125 ppm range. A new tank without any scale protection will see the same mineral buildup as the old one, so we talk through anode rod options and, where it makes sense, pairing the install with a water softener rather than just swapping like for like.

At 6,202 feet, gas appliances also need to be sized and vented for altitude. A unit rated for sea level performance can underperform here if the installation does not account for the thinner air, which is one more reason a straight box swap is not always the right call.

We also check the existing gas line and shutoff valve condition during any replacement quote, since an aging valve or undersized line can limit which units are actually a safe fit, regardless of what the homeowner had planned to install.

Electric units have their own considerations at this elevation, mainly around panel capacity rather than combustion. If a home's electrical panel is already near capacity, switching from gas to an electric or heat pump unit may call for an electrician's input before installation day.

We flag that possibility during the initial quote rather than after the old unit is already disconnected, so there are no surprise delays on installation day. Every installation follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado, including venting and gas piping requirements.

When to Replace

Signs It Is Time

Age Over 10-12 Years

Most tanks are nearing the end of their design life by this point, even without an active failure.

Rising Repair Costs

Multiple repairs in a year usually add up to more than a new unit over time.

Rust in the Water

Discolored hot water is often a sign of internal tank corrosion that cannot be repaired.

Not Enough Hot Water

A household that has grown since the tank was installed may simply need more capacity.

Tank vs. Tankless

Which Fits Your Home

Traditional Tank

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Simpler venting requirements
  • Limited hot water at peak demand
  • Typically 10-15 year lifespan

Tankless

  • Continuous hot water on demand
  • Smaller footprint, wall-mounted
  • Lower standby energy use
  • Typically 20+ year lifespan with descaling
