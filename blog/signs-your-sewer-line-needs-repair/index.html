Signs Your Sewer Line Needs Repair

A small collection of symptoms usually means more than any single one alone.

By the Castle Rock Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026

A sewer line problem rarely announces itself clearly at first. Instead, it shows up as a collection of smaller symptoms that, looked at individually, could mean several different things. Recognizing the pattern early can be the difference between a manageable repair and a full backup into your home.

What to Do If You Have an Active Backup

  • Stop using all water in the house, including toilets, sinks, and washing machines.
  • Avoid walking through or touching standing wastewater directly.
  • Keep children and pets away from the affected area.
  • Photograph any damage for insurance before cleanup begins.
  • Ventilate the area if there is a strong odor.
  • Call for emergency service rather than attempting to clear a main-line backup yourself.

The Warning Signs, in Order of How Often We See Them

Multiple Slow Drains

Every drain in the house running slow at once, not just one fixture, points to the main line rather than an isolated clog.

Gurgling Toilets

A toilet that gurgles when a nearby sink or shower drains is a classic main-line symptom worth taking seriously.

Sewage Odor

A persistent smell near a floor drain, in the yard near the cleanout, or in a specific room can indicate a break or blockage.

Lush or Soggy Yard Patches

An unusually green or wet patch of lawn, especially in dry weather, can mean a leaking sewer lateral is fertilizing the grass above it.

Why Root Intrusion Is Such a Common Cause

Mature trees send roots toward any source of moisture, and a sewer line is a reliable one. Once a root finds even a tiny gap in an aging pipe joint, it grows into that space, gradually widening the gap and eventually creating a significant blockage or structural weak point. This is a slow process, often taking years, which is part of why a home with mature landscaping and an older sewer lateral is at meaningfully higher risk than a newer home with young trees.

Older Pipe Materials Matter Too

Homes near Castle Rock's historic downtown core are more likely to have original clay or cast-iron sewer laterals, both of which are more vulnerable to root intrusion and general deterioration than modern PVC. Clay pipe joints in particular were never designed to be perfectly sealed against root infiltration the way modern solvent-welded PVC joints are. If your home was built before a certain era and you have never had the sewer line inspected, it is a reasonable thing to check proactively rather than waiting for symptoms.

The Same Clay Soil Strikes Again

Castle Rock's expansive Pierre Shale and Denver Formation clay, already a factor in slab leaks, also affects buried sewer lines. As the soil swells and shrinks seasonally, it can shift a sewer lateral's alignment slightly over time, opening joints or creating low spots where solids and paper collect instead of flowing through properly. This is a contributing factor distinct from root intrusion, and a camera inspection can often show a difference between these two failure patterns.

Why a Camera Inspection Comes First

Before recommending a repair method, a proper diagnosis involves running a camera through the line to see exactly what is happening: root intrusion at a specific joint, a bellied or sunken section, a full collapse, or simple grease and debris buildup that snaking alone can resolve. Skipping this step and guessing at a repair approach risks either underspending on a fix that will not last, or overspending on excavation that a less invasive method could have handled.

Repair Options Once the Cause Is Known

Depending on what the camera reveals, options range from hydro jetting to clear buildup and root growth, to a localized spot repair at a specific damaged joint, to trenchless pipe lining for a line with distributed but not catastrophic damage, up to full excavation and replacement for a severely compromised line. Each of these is a genuinely different scope and cost, which is exactly why the camera inspection matters before anyone commits to a specific approach.

What Happens If You Ignore the Early Signs

A sewer line problem does not resolve itself, and delaying diagnosis typically means the eventual repair is larger and more expensive than it would have been earlier. A joint with minor root intrusion, caught early, might be addressed with hydro jetting or a targeted spot repair. The same joint, left untreated for years, can progress to a full blockage or structural collapse requiring excavation. The symptoms described above are worth acting on within days or weeks, not months.

How This Differs From a Septic System Issue

If your property is on a septic system rather than municipal sewer, similar symptoms can point to a different cause entirely, a tank due for pumping or a failing drain field rather than a sewer lateral problem. Most in-town Castle Rock addresses connect to municipal sewer through Castle Rock Water, but properties in unincorporated areas near Castlewood Canyon and Franktown are more likely to be on septic. Knowing which system your property has changes the diagnostic approach considerably.

Trenchless Repair: When It Applies and When It Does Not

Trenchless sewer repair methods, including pipe lining, have become a genuinely useful option for many sewer line problems, avoiding the yard disruption of traditional excavation. They are not universally applicable, however. A line with a full structural collapse, significant misalignment, or certain types of damage may still require traditional digging. This is another reason the camera inspection matters: it tells you honestly which category your specific situation falls into, rather than assuming the less invasive option will work before actually confirming it.

If a trenchless method genuinely fits your situation, it is usually the faster and less disruptive choice. If it does not, a plumber who tells you that honestly upfront saves you the cost and delay of attempting a method that was never going to hold for your specific damage.

If any of the symptoms described here sound familiar, it is worth scheduling a camera inspection now rather than waiting for a full backup to force the issue.
