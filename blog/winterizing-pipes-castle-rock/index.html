Winterizing Your Pipes in Castle Rock

Real steps for our elevation and freeze pattern, not generic national advice.

By the Castle Rock Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Generic winterization advice written for a lower-elevation, milder climate does not fully apply to Castle Rock. At 6,202 feet on the Palmer Divide, our overnight freezes are harder and more frequent than much of the Denver metro below us. Here is what actually matters for our specific conditions.

Why Elevation Changes the Math

Temperature generally drops with elevation, and Castle Rock's position on the Palmer Divide means we see colder overnight lows than Denver proper on many winter nights, even when daytime highs look similar. A pipe that would be borderline-safe at a lower elevation can be a real freeze risk here, which is why insulation standards that work fine in other Front Range suburbs sometimes are not quite enough for Castle Rock specifically.

The Highest-Risk Locations in a Typical Home

Garages

Often unheated or minimally heated, and a common location for a washing machine supply line or water heater.

Crawlspaces

Older homes especially can have supply lines running through unconditioned crawlspace air.

Exterior Walls

A pipe run through an exterior wall with inadequate insulation sits closer to outdoor temperatures than one in an interior wall.

Hose Bibs

Outdoor spigots and the pipe just behind them freeze easily if not properly drained and shut off for the season.

A Real Winterization Checklist

  • Disconnect and drain all garden hoses before the first hard freeze.
  • Shut off interior valves to outdoor spigots if your home has them, and drain the line.
  • Insulate exposed pipes in garages, crawlspaces, and unheated areas with foam pipe sleeves.
  • Seal gaps and cracks near where pipes enter the home from outside.
  • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during extreme cold snaps.
  • Know your main water shutoff valve location before you need it in an emergency.

What to Do Before Winter Travel

If you are leaving town for an extended period during winter, do not turn the heat off entirely to save money. Set the thermostat no lower than the mid-fifties, which keeps interior temperatures high enough to protect most plumbing even during a cold snap while you are away. For an extended absence, consider having someone check the house periodically, or shutting off the main water valve entirely if the home will be fully unoccupied for weeks.

The Specific Danger of a Mild Stretch Followed by a Hard Freeze

One pattern we see repeatedly in Castle Rock is a run of relatively mild weather that lulls homeowners into skipping their usual precautions, followed by a sudden, sharp cold snap. That transition, more than a steady cold winter, is often when we see the most burst pipe calls. A pipe that has been fine all winter can still freeze during that first genuinely hard night if it was never properly insulated.

If a Pipe Freezes Anyway

A frozen pipe that has not yet burst is still an emergency, not a wait-and-see situation. Ice expanding inside a line can split it from the inside, and the break often does not show until the ice thaws and water starts flowing through the damaged section again. Shut off the nearest faucet slightly to relieve pressure if the line is not already leaking, and call for professional thawing rather than using an open flame or an unattended space heater.

Common Questions

For a pipe with a documented history of freezing, yes, heat tape combined with insulation is a reasonable investment. It is not necessary for every pipe in a typical home, just the specific vulnerable runs identified through past experience or inspection.

Sustained temperatures at or below about 20 degrees Fahrenheit for several hours put uninsulated exposed pipes at real risk, though wind chill and how exposed the specific pipe run is both affect the actual threshold.

A slow trickle can relieve pressure in a vulnerable line during an extreme cold snap, but it is a supplement to proper insulation, not a substitute for it.

Getting Ready Before the Next Freeze

If you want a professional assessment of which pipes in your home are genuinely at risk, or if you are dealing with an active frozen pipe right now, call us. If a pipe has already split, our emergency burst pipe repair line runs 24/7 across Castle Rock and Parker.

Attic and Bonus Room Plumbing

Newer Castle Rock homes with bonus rooms or additions built over an unheated garage deserve special attention. Supply lines running through that transition zone between conditioned and unconditioned space see less consistent heat than the rest of the house, and builders do not always insulate this area to the same standard as more visible pipe runs. If your home has a bonus room over a garage, it is worth specifically checking that area's insulation before the first hard freeze rather than assuming standard construction covered it adequately.

Sump Pumps and Winter

If your home has a sump pump, winter is a reasonable time to test it even though sump failures are more commonly associated with spring runoff. A pump that has not run in months can develop issues that only become apparent when it is actually needed, and a frozen discharge line is a real winter-specific failure mode worth checking if your discharge pipe runs above ground before reaching a safe drainage point.

What About Homes on Well Water?

Rural properties near Castlewood Canyon and Franktown on private wells have their own winterization considerations beyond standard household pipes. Well pump housing, pressure tanks, and any exposed supply line between the wellhead and the house all need protection from freezing, and a well system failure in winter can mean a complete loss of water rather than just one affected fixture. If you are on a well and have not had your system's winter-readiness checked, it is worth a call before the season gets fully underway.

A Simple Pre-Winter Timeline

Rather than tackling everything at once, spread winterization tasks across a few weeks each fall. Drain and disconnect hoses as soon as overnight temperatures start regularly dipping near freezing, typically by mid-October in Castle Rock. Insulate exposed pipes and seal gaps before the first hard freeze usually arrives in November. Test your sump pump and confirm your main shutoff valve location during that same window, so everything is ready well before the coldest stretch of winter actually hits.

Doing this a little at a time is far less stressful than trying to remember everything the night before the first forecasted hard freeze.
