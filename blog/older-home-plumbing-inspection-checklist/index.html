Buying an Older Home in Castle Rock: A Plumbing Inspection Checklist

What a standard home inspection often misses, and what to ask instead.

By the Castle Rock Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026

A standard home inspection covers plumbing at a basic level, but it is not the same as a focused plumbing assessment. If you are buying an older home in Castle Rock, particularly one near the historic downtown core, a few targeted questions and checks can reveal issues a general inspector might miss or only note in passing.

Why Older Castle Rock Homes Need Extra Attention

Homes built before certain eras in Castle Rock's historic core can still have original galvanized supply lines, aging cast-iron drain lines, or a sewer lateral that has never been camera-inspected. None of these are automatically dealbreakers, but they are worth knowing about before closing, since discovering them after you own the home means the repair decision and cost are entirely yours.

Questions Worth Asking the Seller

  • Has the sewer line ever been camera-inspected, and do you have that report?
  • Has the home had any slab leaks or foundation repairs, and if so, when?
  • What is the age and type of the water heater, and has it ever been serviced?
  • Are the supply lines original, or has the home been partially or fully repiped?
  • Has the home ever had a burst pipe or significant water damage event?
  • Is the home on municipal water and sewer, or a well and septic system?

What a Pre-Purchase Plumbing Inspection Actually Covers

Beyond what a general home inspector checks, a dedicated plumbing assessment can include a sewer camera inspection, which is not always part of a standard inspection package, a pressure test on the water supply system, and a direct visual assessment of visible pipe material and condition in accessible areas like the basement or crawlspace. This is a worthwhile add-on specifically for homes built before certain construction eras, where original materials are more likely to still be in place.

Signs to Look For Yourself During a Showing

Water Pressure

Run multiple fixtures at once if possible; a significant pressure drop can indicate a supply line issue.

Visible Pipe Material

In the basement or utility area, note whether supply lines look like copper, PEX, or older galvanized steel.

Water Heater Age

The manufacture date is usually on a sticker on the unit, useful for estimating remaining life.

Water Stains

On ceilings, walls, or around fixtures, even old and dry, worth asking about directly.

Why Sewer Line Condition Matters So Much

A sewer line replacement is one of the more expensive plumbing surprises a new homeowner can face, and it is also one of the least visible issues during a standard walkthrough. Root intrusion or a structural problem in an aging clay or cast-iron lateral gives no visible sign until it actually backs up, sometimes years after you have moved in. A camera inspection before closing is inexpensive relative to the cost of an unexpected sewer line replacement discovered after the fact.

If the Home Is on Well and Septic

Properties in unincorporated areas near Castlewood Canyon and Franktown are more likely to be on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal service. If you are considering a property like this, ask specifically about well yield, water quality testing history, and septic tank pumping records. These systems have their own maintenance patterns entirely separate from municipal-connected homes, and a septic system nearing the end of its useful life is a significant cost to inherit unknowingly.

Timing an Inspection With Your Contract

Most real estate contracts include an inspection contingency window, often ranging from a week to two weeks depending on local practice. A dedicated plumbing assessment needs to happen within that window to give you any real negotiating leverage if something significant turns up. Scheduling it as soon as you go under contract, rather than waiting until closer to the deadline, gives you more room to negotiate or reconsider if the results are concerning.

What to Do With a Concerning Result

If a camera inspection reveals root intrusion, a structural issue, or significant deterioration in the sewer line, you generally have a few paths forward: negotiate a price reduction to cover the eventual repair, request the seller complete the repair before closing, or in some cases walk away from the deal if the issue is severe enough relative to your budget. Which path makes sense depends on the specific findings, the overall deal terms, and how much you want the property despite the finding.

Documentation to Request From the Seller

Beyond verbal answers to your questions, ask for any documentation the seller actually has: past service invoices from plumbers, permit records for any past repiping or major repair work, and septic pumping records if applicable. A seller who has kept good records and can produce them is a positive signal about how the home has been maintained overall, not just for plumbing specifically.

A Note on New Construction Too

While this guide focuses on older homes, newer Castle Rock construction is not automatically free of plumbing concerns either. A pre-drywall inspection during the building process, if your builder allows it, can catch installation issues before they are sealed behind walls. For a completed new build you are purchasing, a final walkthrough plumbing check before your final closing is still worth including, even though the risk profile is different from an older resale home.

Whether the property is a century-old home near Mainstreet or a brand-new build in a newer subdivision, the underlying principle is the same: a real plumbing assessment before you own the problem is far cheaper than discovering it afterward.
