What Counts as a Plumbing Emergency? A Homeowner's Guide

Knowing the difference saves money and prevents unnecessary damage.

By the Castle Rock Pro Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Not every plumbing problem is an emergency, and not every emergency feels urgent in the moment. Knowing the actual distinction can save you both money on unnecessary emergency dispatch fees and real damage from waiting too long on something that genuinely could not wait.

What Actually Qualifies as an Emergency

Burst or Actively Leaking Pipe

Water flowing where it should not be, especially in volume, cannot wait for a scheduled appointment.

Sewage Backup

A health hazard as much as a plumbing problem, this needs immediate attention.

No Water at All

A complete loss of water supply, not just reduced pressure, especially affects daily function significantly.

Gas Smell Near a Water Heater

This is both a plumbing and safety emergency; leave the area and call your gas utility's emergency line immediately, then a plumber.

What Usually Is Not an Emergency

A Slow Drain

Annoying, but rarely urgent unless it is a sign of a larger backup developing.

A Dripping Faucet

Wastes water and money over time but is not causing active damage right now.

A Running Toilet

Similarly a waste-of-water issue rather than an active damage risk in most cases.

Low Water Pressure

Frustrating, but usually not something that gets meaningfully worse by waiting a day or two for a scheduled visit.

The Gray Area Cases

Some situations genuinely sit in between. A water heater that has stopped producing hot water is inconvenient but rarely dangerous, making it reasonable to schedule rather than treat as an emergency, unless you have a specific reason it cannot wait, like a medical need for hot water. A slow leak that has been dripping for a while and just started pooling visibly is worth treating with more urgency than a truly minor issue, even if it is not actively flooding a room, since the underlying cause is clearly still active.

Why This Distinction Actually Matters

Emergency dispatch, whether from us or any plumbing company, typically carries a premium over a scheduled appointment, reflecting the real cost of having technicians available outside normal hours. Calling for genuine emergencies preserves that resource for situations that actually need it, and it also means faster response for you specifically, since a true emergency call gets prioritized appropriately rather than competing with routine work.

What to Do While Deciding

If you are not sure which category your situation falls into, calling and describing what is happening is a reasonable first step, even if it turns out not to be an emergency. A good plumbing company will tell you honestly whether your situation needs immediate response or can be scheduled, rather than pushing every call toward the more expensive emergency option regardless of actual urgency.

Castle Rock-Specific Emergency Patterns

Certain emergencies cluster seasonally here. Winter brings a real spike in burst and frozen pipe calls tied to our elevation and freeze pattern. Spring brings more sump pump and basement flooding emergencies tied to runoff. Being aware of these patterns can help you recognize when a symptom that might otherwise seem minor deserves more urgency given the season, like a basement dampness starting to appear right as spring runoff typically peaks.

If You Smell Gas Near a Water Heater

  • Do not touch any electrical switches, including light switches, in the area.
  • Do not attempt to locate the leak yourself or investigate the appliance.
  • Leave the house immediately and get everyone else out as well.
  • Call your gas utility's emergency line from outside the home, not from inside.
  • Call a plumber once the gas utility has confirmed it is safe to return.
  • Do not re-enter the home until you have been told it is safe to do so.

Why We List This Separately

A gas leak is fundamentally different from every other item on this list because it is not just a plumbing or water problem, it is a genuine safety hazard that can affect the whole household immediately. Treating it with the same urgency as a slow drain would be a serious mistake. If you are ever uncertain whether a smell near a gas appliance is significant, treat it as significant and act accordingly rather than trying to assess it yourself.

After-Hours vs. Weekend vs. Holiday Response

Emergency response is generally available regardless of time or day, but it is worth understanding that dispatch fees and availability can vary based on exactly when you call. A weeknight emergency, a weekend emergency, and a major holiday emergency are not always treated identically by every company, and it is reasonable to ask directly what to expect when you call, rather than assuming a flat rate applies no matter when the issue occurs.

Keeping a Household Emergency Plan

Beyond knowing what qualifies as an emergency, it helps to have a basic plan in place: knowing your main shutoff valve location, having a plumber's number saved rather than needing to search for one during a crisis, and knowing your gas utility's emergency contact information as well. None of this takes long to put together, and having it ready before you need it removes one more source of stress during an already stressful situation.

The goal is not to make every homeowner anxious about every drip or slow drain, but to build genuine confidence about when something actually needs immediate action versus when it can reasonably wait for a scheduled visit.

If you are ever unsure, err on the side of calling and asking rather than guessing on your own, especially for anything involving gas or significant active water flow.
