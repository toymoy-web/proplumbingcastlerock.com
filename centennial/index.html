Local Cause

Why Plumbing Needs Vary by Area Here

Centennial's age of housing stock varies more block-to-block than in a single-era planned community, since the city was assembled from existing unincorporated neighborhoods (formerly the Castlewood and Southglenn areas) rather than built out from a single master plan. That means a 1970s home in one Centennial neighborhood can sit blocks from 2000s infill, each with a different plumbing generation to account for.

We ask about the approximate build year on every Centennial service call for exactly this reason, since it tells us more about likely pipe material and water heater age here than it would in a single-era community like Highlands Ranch.

Centennial's origin as an assembled city, formed from the former Castlewood and Southglenn areas of unincorporated Arapahoe County, is also why its own name occasionally causes confusion with the unrelated Castlewood Canyon area southeast of Castle Rock, a completely different place despite the similar name.

Arapahoe County, not Douglas County, governs building permits for most Centennial addresses, a distinction worth knowing if you are comparing notes with a Castle Rock or Douglas County neighbor about how a similar project was permitted.

We coordinate that permitting difference on our end so it is not something you need to research yourself before scheduling a larger job. Every repair follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.

Our routes also cover the corridor toward Highlands Ranch's 80126 and back to Castle Rock's 80104, so Centennial calls run on the same regular schedule as the rest of our coverage area.

The Streets at SouthGlenn area in particular sees a mix of newer mixed-use residential and older single-family homes within a few blocks, another reason we ask about build era rather than assuming based on the neighborhood name alone.

We would rather ask one extra question upfront than show up assuming the wrong plumbing generation for your specific block.

That same attention applies to scheduling. A Southglenn service call and a Piney Creek service call get the same response time and the same upfront quote process, regardless of which part of Centennial you are in.

Call us with your address and we can tell you what to generally expect before a technician even arrives.

Flood-Zone Properties

What to Do Near Cherry Creek or the High Line Canal

  • Know whether your address falls in a FEMA-designated flood zone before an event happens, not during one.
  • Test your sump pump before spring runoff and summer storm season each year.
  • Keep valuables and stored items elevated off basement floors in flood-prone sections.
  • Photograph any water damage immediately for insurance before cleanup begins.
  • Know your main water shutoff location in case a backflow event occurs.
  • Call promptly rather than waiting to see if water recedes on its own.
