Signs You Need Service

What Homeowners Actually Notice First

Few people call us asking for a descale by name. More often it is a unit that used to deliver instant hot water and now takes a while to warm up, or a display flashing a code the homeowner has never seen before. Some notice the water temperature swinging while running the dishwasher and shower at the same time.

Each of those is a sign the heat exchanger needs attention, not that the unit is failing outright. Left long enough, though, scale buildup can shorten a tankless unit's life well below its rated 20-plus years.

A unit installed without a service valve kit also makes future descaling harder and more expensive than it needs to be. If your tankless heater was installed without one, we can add it during a service visit so future maintenance is faster.

We also see units short-cycling, meaning the burner fires on and off rapidly instead of maintaining a steady flame, which is often traced back to a flow sensor affected by the same scale buildup rather than a failing burner assembly.

Ignoring these symptoms rarely resolves on its own. A tankless unit running on a scaled heat exchanger works harder for the same output, which shows up eventually as a higher gas bill even before an outright failure occurs.

We also inspect the inlet water filter during every service call, since a clogged filter can mimic several of the same symptoms as a scaled heat exchanger, and clearing it first can save you the cost of a full descale you did not actually need.

We document the inlet filter and heat exchanger condition at every visit, giving you a real maintenance history to reference for warranty claims later.

Local Cause

Why This Matters More in Castle Rock

Castle Rock Water reports moderately hard to hard water in the 108 to 125 ppm range. Every gallon that passes through a tankless heat exchanger leaves a little mineral scale behind, and because a tankless unit heats water on demand through narrow passages, that scale reduces efficiency faster than it would in a standard tank.

Homes on the higher end of that hardness range, or households running multiple fixtures at once regularly, may benefit from a more frequent descale schedule than the manufacturer's default recommendation assumes.
