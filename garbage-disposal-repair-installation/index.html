Signs You Need a Repair

Common Disposal Issues

Humming, No Spin

Usually a jam that a reset button and manual crank can often clear.

Leaking Underneath

Often a failed seal or a cracked housing that needs replacement.

Frequent Breaker Trips

Can mean the motor is failing or the unit is undersized for household use.

Unusual Grinding Noise

May indicate an object lodged against the impeller plate.

Local Cause

A Note on Local Water Conditions

Castle Rock's moderately hard to hard water does not damage a disposal directly, but mineral buildup in the drain line just past the unit can create backups that get mistaken for a disposal problem. We check the connected drain line as part of any disposal service call for this reason.

A disposal that smells bad even after running is often a sign of trapped food debris rather than a mechanical fault, and a proper cleaning can resolve it without any parts replacement at all.

We also check the dishwasher air-gap or knockout plug connection during any disposal installation, since a missed knockout on a new unit is a common and easily avoided cause of a dishwasher leak right after a disposal swap.

Rental and multi-unit properties in Castle Rock also see a distinct pattern: disposals used more heavily than a typical single-family household, which is worth factoring into horsepower selection when a landlord asks us to replace one.

We can also advise property managers on which models hold up best under high turnover use, since a unit that seems fine for a single owner-occupant may need more frequent service in a rental with a new tenant every year or two.

If you are unsure whether your current unit is worth repairing, tell us the brand and roughly how old it is when you call. That alone often narrows down whether a quick repair or a replacement is the more sensible path forward.

A brief description of the noise it makes, or lack of one, also helps us bring the right tools on the first visit, since a silent jam and a humming motor point toward different likely causes before we ever open the cabinet.

A quick description over the phone genuinely does save time on-site, both for you and for us. All disposal connections follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.
