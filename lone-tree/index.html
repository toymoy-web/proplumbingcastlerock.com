New Construction Focus

Why Lone Tree's Plumbing Skews New

Lone Tree's first subdivision opened in 1982 with just nine households, and the city has grown in phases ever since, most recently through the 3,500-acre RidgeGate development east and west of I-25. That means a larger share of Lone Tree's plumbing is recent PEX construction compared to Castle Rock's mix of older and newer homes, though original 1980s-1990s sections still exist and are now old enough for their first major repairs.

New-construction issues we see most in Lone Tree include builder-grade fixture failures still under warranty, rough-in inspection punch lists, and fixture upgrades homeowners choose to make within the first few years of moving in, alongside the usual repair calls from established sections of the city.

The RidgeGate East Village, still actively building out with light rail access and new residential blocks, adds a further wrinkle: some streets are genuinely brand new, with plumbing generations only a year or two old sitting near the community's original 1980s core.

Sky Ridge Medical Center and the growing commercial corridor around it also mean a real mix of residential and commercial calls in the same general area, unlike a purely residential section of Castle Rock.

Lone Tree's home rule city status, established in 1998, also means its own building department rather than relying on Douglas County directly, worth knowing if you are coordinating a larger project's permitting timeline.

We handle that city-level permitting process the same way we handle Castle Rock's, so it is one less thing for a homeowner or contractor to coordinate separately.

Whether your address is in the original 1982 core or a brand-new RidgeGate East street, the same crew, the same pricing, and the same 24/7 line apply.

We do not treat a brand-new build as somehow lower priority than an older service call, or the reverse. Both get the same diagnostic-first approach before any work begins.

That consistency matters in a city growing as fast as Lone Tree, where the mix of old and new keeps shifting every year as RidgeGate continues to build out. Every repair follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.

A Note on Lone Tree's Water Providers

Lone Tree is served by a mix of providers depending on the section, including Denver Water, Southgate Water and Sanitation District, and Rampart Range Metropolitan District in newer RidgeGate-area developments. Confirming which applies to a specific address matters more here than in a single-utility city like Castle Rock.
