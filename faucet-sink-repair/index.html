Faucet & Sink Repair in Castle Rock, CO

A dripping faucet, a loose valve, or a sink that will not shut off completely wastes water and often points to a worn cartridge or valve. We repair or replace the part, not the whole fixture, whenever that is the honest fix.

Signs You Need a Repair

Common Faucet & Sink Issues

Persistent Dripping

Usually a worn cartridge, washer, or O-ring inside the faucet body.

Low or Uneven Flow

Can mean a clogged aerator or a failing valve, not a supply issue.

Loose or Wobbly Faucet

Often a mounting nut issue rather than a sign the fixture needs replacing.

Leak Under the Sink

Supply line or P-trap connections are the most common culprits.

Local Cause

Why Castle Rock's Water Matters Here

Castle Rock Water reports moderately hard to hard water in the 108 to 125 ppm range, which accelerates mineral buildup inside faucet cartridges and aerators. A faucet that drips or feels stiff to turn is often dealing with scale on the internal parts well before the fixture itself is actually worn out.

This is why we check the water conditions in play before recommending a fix. A household already running a water softener sees far less of this buildup than one without any treatment, and that context changes what a lasting repair actually looks like.

Pull-down kitchen faucets and single-handle bathroom faucets each have their own common failure points, and a technician familiar with both saves a second visit caused by guessing at the wrong part on the first one.

Two-handle bathroom faucets, common in Castle Rock homes built before the mid-2000s, use a different valve style entirely, and parts for older discontinued models sometimes take an extra day to source.

We keep a range of common cartridges stocked on the truck for exactly this reason, so most repairs on mainstream brands are finished in the same visit rather than requiring a return trip.

For a full kitchen or bath remodel involving multiple fixtures, we can quote the whole scope at once rather than pricing each faucet and sink separately. All fixture work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.

Repair vs. Replace

When to Repair, When to Replace

Worth Repairing

  • Fixture under 10 years old
  • Single worn part identified
  • No visible corrosion on the body
  • Repair cost well under a new fixture

Time to Replace

  • Fixture is discontinued or unbranded
  • Multiple failed parts over time
  • Visible corrosion or finish failure
  • Homeowner wants a style or finish change
