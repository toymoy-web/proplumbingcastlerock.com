Signs You Need It

When Snaking Is Not Enough

Most calls for hydro jetting start as a call about something else. A homeowner mentions the line was already snaked once or twice this year, or that a company tried to clear it but the cable got stuck partway through. Some describe recurring backups that clear temporarily, then return within a few weeks at the same spot.

Any of these usually points to buildup or root intrusion along the full length of the pipe rather than a single localized clog, which is exactly what hydro jetting is built to address.

We confirm this with a camera before recommending jetting rather than assuming, since a genuinely collapsed or offset pipe section needs repair, not just cleaning, and jetting a badly damaged line can make things worse.

We also size the jetting nozzle and pressure to the pipe diameter and material rather than using one setting for every job, which is part of why a proper camera inspection comes first on every call, in line with International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards for sewer and drain work.

A line that has never been jetted before often shows the most dramatic improvement, since years of gradual buildup are removed in a single visit rather than chipped away gradually by repeated snaking.

We can also set up a periodic jetting schedule for properties with a known recurring issue, such as an older shared line serving multiple units, so buildup never gets far enough to cause a backup in the first place.

Local Cause

Why This Comes Up More in Castle Rock

Mature trees in established neighborhoods send roots toward the moisture inside older clay or cast-iron sewer laterals, and once a root finds a small gap in a joint, it keeps growing back after simple snaking. Grease and scale buildup add to the same problem in kitchen and main lines over years of normal use.

Commercial kitchens and older multi-family properties in Castle Rock see this pattern especially often, where grease accumulation in a shared line builds up faster than any single tenant would notice from their own fixtures.

Method Comparison

Snaking vs. Hydro Jetting

Cable Snaking

  • Punches through a single blockage
  • Faster for a simple, isolated clog
  • Leaves residue on pipe walls
  • Does not remove root growth

Hydro Jetting

  • Clears the full pipe diameter
  • Removes grease, scale, and root growth
  • Longer-lasting result
  • Better for recurring or main-line issues
