Signs You Might Have One

What Homeowners Actually Notice

Few callers say "I think I have a leak" right away. More often it starts with a water bill that jumped without an obvious cause, a meter that is still spinning with every faucet off, or a damp patch on a wall or ceiling with no clear source. Some notice a faint sound of running water somewhere in the house when nothing is turned on.

Any of those on their own is worth a call. Together, they usually mean a real leak somewhere between the meter and the fixture, not a billing error or a fluke.

We also see cases where a homeowner assumed a high bill was a utility error and disputed it, only to find months later that a real leak had been running the entire time. Confirming or ruling out a leak early avoids that wasted time.

Castle Rock Water's own billing data can be a useful early signal too. A sudden jump compared to the same month in prior years, without a change in household habits, is often the first concrete evidence that something has changed underground. Any repair we recommend follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.

Local Cause

Why This Comes Up Often in Castle Rock

Expansive Pierre Shale and Denver Formation clay under much of Castle Rock puts uneven stress on buried water lines as the soil swells and shrinks with moisture. That stress is a common cause of hidden leaks that never show up as an obvious puddle, since the water simply disperses into the surrounding soil before it ever reaches the surface.

Where Leaks Hide

Common Leak Locations

Under the Slab

Supply lines running beneath the foundation, often tied to soil movement.

Behind Walls

Interior wall runs near bathrooms and kitchens are common failure points.

At the Water Meter

The service line between the meter and the home can leak underground.

Around Fixtures

Toilets and water heaters can leak slowly enough to go unnoticed for months.
