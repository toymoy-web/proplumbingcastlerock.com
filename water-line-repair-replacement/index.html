Signs You Might Have This Issue

What Points to the Service Line

The water service line runs underground between the street or meter and the house, and problems there show up differently than an inside-the-wall leak. A homeowner usually notices reduced pressure at every fixture at once, a soggy patch of yard along the line's likely path, or a water bill that has climbed with no visible leak anywhere inside.

Any of those, especially combined, is worth investigating the service line specifically rather than assuming the issue is somewhere inside the home.

Local Cause

Why This Comes Up in Castle Rock

The same Pierre Shale and Denver Formation clay that stresses interior plumbing under a slab also puts pressure on the buried service line itself as the soil swells and shrinks seasonally. Older galvanized service lines, more common on properties near Castle Rock's historic downtown, are also prone to internal corrosion that restricts flow well before an outright failure.

Because this line sits deep enough to stay below the frost line, a failure here is rarely a sudden freeze event the way an interior pipe break can be. It is almost always gradual corrosion or shifting soil pressure building up over years.

We coordinate with Castle Rock Water on any work near the meter itself, since the meter and its immediate connection are typically utility property even though the line beyond it is the homeowner's responsibility.

A neighbor's recent line replacement is sometimes the first clue a homeowner has that similarly aged infrastructure on their own property may be approaching the same point, since properties built in the same era and phase often share pipe material and installation date. All work follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.

We can review your property's build date and, where records are available, the subdivision's typical service line material, to give you a realistic sense of remaining life before you have a problem, not just after.

Repair Options

Trenchless vs. Traditional Dig

Traditional Excavation

  • Full access to the damaged section
  • Needed for severe collapse or corrosion
  • More yard or driveway disruption
  • Can cost less for short, shallow runs

Trenchless Replacement

  • Minimal digging, mostly access pits
  • Good fit for a full-length line replacement
  • Faster restoration of the yard
  • Not right for every situation
