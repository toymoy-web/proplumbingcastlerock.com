Common Issues Here

What Property Type Changes

Most calls we get from the Castlewood Canyon and Franktown area start differently than a typical in-town Castle Rock call: a septic tank overdue for pumping, a private well pump acting up, or a rural property's plumbing needing service after years without a municipal inspection cycle to catch small issues early.

This is unincorporated Douglas County land, near the historic 1933 Castlewood Dam site and the Highway 83/86 intersection at Franktown, and it is genuinely different from in-town Castle Rock plumbing in ways that go beyond just distance from downtown.

A well pump that has run continuously for irrigation and household use both can wear differently than a purely domestic setup, and a septic system serving a larger equestrian property sees different demands than a standard suburban lot. We ask about both when scheduling a call out here.

Access can also take longer than an in-town call, since gravel roads and larger setbacks between the road and the house are common on these acreage properties, something worth mentioning when you call so we can plan accordingly.

We would rather ask a few extra questions upfront about your property's layout and water source than show up without the right equipment for a well-and-septic setup, which is a real difference from a standard in-town service call.

If you have recently purchased a rural property out here and are still learning its systems, we are happy to walk through the basics of your well and septic setup during a first visit, not just fix the immediate issue and leave.

A few extra minutes explaining how your specific setup works costs nothing and gives you a real reference point for next time, rather than starting from zero again with whoever you call.

Out here, a good working relationship with a plumber who already knows your property tends to matter more than it would for a standard in-town home.

Local Governance

A Note on Permitting Here

Properties in this area fall under unincorporated Douglas County, not the Town of Castle Rock, for permitting purposes. Well and septic systems here are typically permitted separately from municipal water and sewer connections, and any real estate transaction in the area usually involves verifying well yield and septic condition, since neither is guaranteed the way a municipal hookup would be.
