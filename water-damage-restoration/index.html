What to Do During Water Damage

  • Stop the water source if it is safe to do so, such as shutting off the main valve.
  • Turn off electricity to affected areas before walking through standing water.
  • Move furniture, rugs, and electronics away from the wet area if possible.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurance claim before cleanup begins.
  • Do not run a fan or HVAC system that could spread mold spores if damage is more than 24 to 48 hours old.
  • Call our water damage line rather than waiting to see if it dries on its own.
What This Service Covers

Restoration, Not Just the Plumbing Fix

Water damage restoration addresses the cleanup, drying, and structural side of an event, such as extracting standing water, drying out framing and drywall, and addressing early mold risk. This is a distinct service from the plumbing repair itself. A burst pipe repair fixes the pipe; water damage restoration addresses everything the water touched afterward.

We can coordinate both under one visit when a job calls for it, but we will always be upfront about which part of the invoice is the plumbing repair and which is the restoration work, rather than bundling them into one unclear total. Any plumbing repair involved still follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.

Castle Rock's basement-heavy housing stock, common across neighborhoods from The Meadows to Founders Village, means a sump pump failure or a slab leak can affect a large finished living area quickly. The sooner drying starts, the less material typically needs to be replaced.

Category matters too. Clean water from a supply line is handled differently than water from a sewer backup, which carries contamination risk and requires a different cleanup and disposal approach for affected materials.

Finished basements add another layer of complexity, since drywall, carpet pad, and baseboards behind furniture can trap moisture that is not obvious from a quick look at the room. A proper assessment checks behind and underneath finished surfaces, not just the visible wet spot.

We use moisture meters to confirm drying progress rather than guessing by feel, and we do not consider a job finished until readings in affected materials return to a normal range.
