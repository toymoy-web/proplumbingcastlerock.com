Signs You Might Have One

What a Slab Leak Actually Feels Like

Most homeowners do not call us asking for a slab leak repair by name. They call because a section of flooring feels warm for no reason, or because the water bill jumped and nothing in the home explains it. Some describe a damp or musty patch on the carpet near an interior wall, or a faint sound of running water when every fixture in the house is off.

Any of those on their own is worth a call. Together, they are close to a textbook slab leak, and the earlier it is traced, the less concrete work the repair usually needs.

Local Cause

Why This Happens More in Castle Rock

Much of Castle Rock sits over Pierre Shale and Denver Formation clay, the same expansive soil documented from Founders Village to Crystal Valley. As that clay absorbs moisture and swells, then dries and shrinks, it puts uneven pressure on the copper or PEX supply lines running under a slab foundation. Over years, that stress is what actually opens the leak, not just pipe age alone.

This is also why two homes built the same year on the same street can have very different slab leak histories. A home on a lot with better drainage away from the foundation sees less moisture cycling in the clay beneath it, while a home where water pools near the slab after storms goes through that swell-and-shrink cycle more aggressively, year after year.

Repair Options

Direct Repair vs. Reroute

Direct Slab Repair

  • Opens the slab at the exact leak point
  • Lower cost for a single, accessible leak
  • Concrete patch required after
  • Best for one clearly located leak

Pipe Reroute

  • Runs new line through walls or attic
  • Avoids cutting the slab at all
  • Better for aging pipe with multiple risk points
  • Often preferred on older copper systems
Any direct repair or reroute we recommend follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado, and every quote is confirmed before work begins.
