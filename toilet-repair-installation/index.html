Signs You Need a Repair

Common Toilet Issues

Running Constantly

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve, an easy and inexpensive fix.

Weak or Incomplete Flush

Can mean a clogged trapway, low tank water level, or a mineral-scaled jet.

Rocking or Wobbling

Often a failed wax ring or loose closet bolts, not a sign to replace the toilet.

Water at the Base

A leaking wax ring seal, which can lead to subfloor damage if left too long.

Local Cause

Why Castle Rock's Water Matters Here

Castle Rock Water's moderately hard to hard water, in the 108 to 125 ppm range, contributes to mineral scale inside the tank and around the rim jets, which is a common cause of a weakening flush over time that has nothing to do with a clog.

Older toilets, particularly those installed before low-flow standards became common, can also simply be undersized for modern water pressure and flush dynamics, which sometimes gets misread as a mechanical failure rather than a design limitation.

A toilet that leaks at the base intermittently, rather than constantly, can also point to a slightly warped flange rather than the wax ring itself, which changes the actual fix needed.

We also see condensation on the tank exterior mistaken for a leak, especially in humid summer months, which is a cosmetic issue rather than a plumbing failure and does not need any parts replaced.

A toilet insulation liner or a tray beneath the tank can help manage that condensation for households bothered by it, a simple accessory fix rather than a repair, but one we are happy to point out during a service visit.

When in doubt, describe what you are seeing when you call rather than guessing at the cause yourself. A running sound, a slow leak, and a wobble each point us toward a different starting diagnosis before we even arrive.

Photos help too, especially of the area around the base or tank if there is visible water, since they let us bring the right parts on the first visit rather than diagnosing everything from scratch once we arrive.

Repair vs. Replace

When to Replace Instead

Worth Repairing

  • Fixture under 15 years old
  • Single worn part identified
  • No cracks in the tank or bowl
  • Repair cost well under a new unit

Time to Replace

  • Visible crack in porcelain
  • Older high-flow model, high water use
  • Repeated repairs over time
  • Wanting a modern, efficient model
