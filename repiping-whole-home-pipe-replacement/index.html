Signs You Need It

When Repair Stops Making Sense

Nobody wants to repipe a house on a whim. The homeowners who end up calling us usually have a pattern behind them: two or three unrelated pipe leaks in the same year, discolored water that will not clear up, or a home built with galvanized steel supply lines that are now decades past their expected lifespan.

On their own, any one of those might just need a repair. Together, they usually mean the pipe material itself is failing throughout the system, not at one unlucky spot.

We also see this conversation come up during a kitchen or bathroom remodel, when opening a wall reveals pipe condition the homeowner had no way of knowing about beforehand, which changes the scope of the project before it even starts.

If that happens mid-remodel, we can usually scope a partial repipe limited to the areas already open, rather than requiring a full whole-home project you had not planned or budgeted for.

We document the existing pipe condition with photos before removal, which has proven useful more than once for a homeowner working through an insurance claim tied to prior water damage from the old system.

A repipe is also a reasonable time to add or relocate shutoff valves for individual fixtures, small upgrades that cost little extra during construction but save real hassle on future repairs.

The peace of mind is real too. Once a full repipe is finished, there is no more wondering which unseen section of the old system might be the next one to fail. Every repipe follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted by Colorado.

Local Cause

Why This Comes Up in Castle Rock

Homes near Castle Rock's older, historic downtown core can still have original galvanized supply lines, which corrode from the inside out over decades and eventually restrict flow before they fail outright. The same expansive clay soil that stresses buried lines under a slab also adds cumulative stress to supply lines throughout a home's structure over many years, which is a real factor in repeat-leak households even in newer construction.

Material Options

PEX vs. Copper

Copper

  • Long track record in residential use
  • Rigid, more resistant to punctures
  • Higher material cost
  • More labor-intensive to install

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer joints needed
  • Faster installation, less wall opening
  • Freeze-resistant flexibility
  • Lower overall material cost
